# Mei — Dialogue

Mei arrives first and alone, carrying the damaged family tin.

- **S2-03:** I'm hoping you can remake something. My grandmother's mooncake.
- **S2-05:** I wish. Grandmother died last winter. Jian was meant to keep the recipe, but now he says he cannot find it.
- **S2-07:** Lotus. I used to grind the soaked seeds while Grandmother watched.
- **S2-09:** She added one small spoonful of sugar. Never more.
- **S2-13:** No. At least... I don't remember one. Jian insists there was a yolk, but he remembers the things he ate, not the things he made.
- **S2-15:** You sound like Grandmother.
- **S2-17:** There was a floral smell. It clung to her sleeves and the paper around every cake.
- **S2-19:** Osmanthus. She tucked a sprig behind my ear while the cakes cooled.
- **S2-21:** This is our first festival without her. I thought if it tasted the same, the house might feel less empty.
- **S3-03:** It was in the cupboard where you left it three years ago.
- **S3-05:** By forgetting it existed?
- **S3-08:** You said red bean last week.
- **S3-09A:** You told me it was somewhere safe.
- **S3-15:** I don't remember that.
- **S3-17:** I was not fighting it.
- **S3-19:** I was eight.
- **S3-24:** You remember that too?
- **S3-26:** Once.
- **S5-F2:** I can still feel those lotus seeds slipping beneath the stone.
- **S5-S1:** That's sweeter than hers. The sugar is hiding the lotus.
- **S5-O1:** The scent is wrong. Grandmother smelled like the tree outside her kitchen.
- **S6-03 — Young Mei:** Jian finished his part already.
- **S6-09 — Young Mei:** Only if he asks nicely.
- **S6-11 — Young Mei:** Accepted.
- **S7-02:** I thought you were changing her recipe to hide that you lost it.
- **S7-04:** I couldn't remember your part, so I treated it as if it never happened.
- **S7-06:** I blamed you because being angry was easier than saying she's gone.
- **S7-09:** Almost.
- **S7-13:** I'll keep the tin.
- **S7-15:** We'll both keep copies.

## Performance

Precise and restrained at first. Her anger at Jian protects her from confronting Grandmother's death. “Almost” should be soft and painful, but not hopeless.
