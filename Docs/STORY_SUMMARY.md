# Story Summary — The Last Mooncake

## Tone

Warm and bittersweet. Recovering the recipe gives the characters a way to carry their loss, but it cannot restore their grandmother or reproduce the past perfectly.

## Main story

Long ago, Artemis served Chang'e, the moon goddess. Chang'e entrusted her with an old mooncake mold and taught her that memories are like moonlight: they may fade from sight, but traces remain in the things people share. Artemis later lost her memories and became trapped as a stray cat.

The Mid-Autumn moon is unusually dim. Artemis shelters in an elderly baker's shop and touches a cracked mooncake mold. A distant memory returns: “When people forget, help them remember.”

The first customer is Lina Chen, a florist who has returned after five years away. This short, guided story acts as the gameplay tutorial. Lina wants to bring her estranged father the mooncake he adapted for her as a child: red bean paste, no yolk, less sugar, and an osmanthus mark. Making it does not reconcile them automatically; it gives Lina the courage to knock on his door. Her story teaches Artemis—and the player—that changing a tradition can be a way of caring for someone.

After Lina leaves, the first Mid-Autumn Festival since the twins' grandmother died becomes the main story.

Mei arrives alone with her grandmother's dented mooncake tin. She wants to recreate the family mooncake, but only remembers lotus paste, very little sugar, and osmanthus. She blames her twin brother Jian, who was meant to preserve the written recipe.

Jian arrives separately looking for the tin. He admits that he lost the recipe while clearing their grandmother's home and was too ashamed to tell Mei. He remembers the missing detail: Grandmother let him place one salted egg yolk in the centre and called it his “little moon.”

The twins argue because Mei thinks Jian is inventing details to excuse his mistake, while Jian believes Mei has decided that only her memories matter. Artemis realizes neither twin ever held the complete recipe. Mei prepared the lotus filling; Jian placed the yolk; Grandmother added the osmanthus and guided them both.

The player reconstructs the mooncake with lotus paste, one salted yolk, less sugar, and osmanthus. The correct cake reveals their shared childhood. Grandmother tells them, “A family recipe is too heavy for one pair of hands,” then adds, “One day, you'll make this without me.” The memory ends before the children can answer.

The recreated cake tastes familiar, but not identical. It cannot bring Grandmother back, and some details are gone forever. Jian apologizes for losing the recipe. Mei admits anger was easier than accepting their grandmother's death. Jian says, “It tastes like her kitchen.” Mei answers, “Almost.”

They write down what they can remember together. Mei leaves carrying the old tin; Jian carries the new recipe. Neither carries the whole memory alone.

Their story restores part of Artemis's past. Chang'e tells her, “To remember someone is not to keep them unchanged. It is to carry what remains, knowing something will always be missing.” Artemis remembers Chang'e's name, but not how they were separated.

The moon grows brighter without becoming completely full. The mold continues to glow, but its crack remains. As Artemis watches the twins leave together, she thinks: “The past does not return. It leaves us enough light to follow.”

## Narrative rules

- Lina is a clear, low-pressure tutorial. Her four clues follow the order of the four recipe controls and are presented one at a time.
- The tutorial ends with the possibility of reconciliation, not a guaranteed happy outcome.
- The mooncake creates space for grief and reconciliation; it does not magically repair loss.
- “Almost” is the emotional key: the recreation is meaningful because it is incomplete, not because it perfectly restores the past.
- The recipe survives by changing from one person's memory into a record the twins make together.
- Artemis gains purpose and one memory, but she does not regain her identity or find her way home yet.
- End with partial light and a visible crack rather than complete restoration.
